# Guest House Pattern

The Guest House (GH) is the compact first-stage complete home and later an
independent or semi-independent campus dwelling. Approximately 1,000 ft² is a
planning baseline, not a hard requirement.

The GH is **not** a miniature Main House. Its profile spends space reluctantly:
provide complete comfortable capability with low permanent spatial burden while
protecting accessibility, privacy, sleep, serviceability, calm, and ordinary
low-friction use.

## Scope

- Profile: `GH`
- Human-occupied stories: **one**
- No design may depend on stairs, a second occupied story, or inter-floor
  transfer/lift infrastructure.
- Vertical volume inside the one-story envelope may still be used for qualified
  storage, cat circulation, deployable capability, service infrastructure, and
  other non-occupied functions when structure, code, accessibility, clearances,
  and lifecycle burden permit.

## Required Capabilities

- safe sleeping, bathing, sanitation, cooking, thermal comfort, and daily living
- accessible egress and circulation
- independent identity and digital-twin address
- clear utility interfaces to the property/campus backbone
- isolation points for power, water, waste, HVAC, communications, and security
  where present
- service access that does not require entering unrelated private spaces
- useful storage without making essential daily use depend on ladders or
  strength-intensive handling
- protected restorative/negative space despite the compact footprint

## Spatial Defaults

- Avoid conventional circulation-only hallways unless code, accessibility,
  privacy, acoustics, geometry, or another protected requirement justifies one.
- Prefer direct room connections or useful transition space when they remain
  legible, private enough, and accessible.
- Cluster wet/service functions tightly where practical.
- Prefer reach-in, full-height, built-in, wall-depth, pull-out, or other compact
  storage before walk-ins.
- Share intermittent capability only when conversion is easy enough that normal
  life does not become setup/teardown work.
- Use deployable/mobile capability more aggressively than MH when it materially
  reduces permanent encumbrance.
- Favor simple shell geometry, short utility runs, low corner/penetration count,
  and standardized dimensions/openings when adequate.
- Minimize duplicate rooms/equipment unless concurrent demand, resilience, or
  convenience actually earns the burden.

## Explicit Non-Goals

The GH must not become a tiny-house puzzle. Do not require daily furniture
Tetris, repeated awkward transformations, inaccessible upper storage, reduced
sleep quality, poor privacy/acoustics, or strength-intensive handling merely to
reduce square footage.

The GH also does not need to absorb every capability eventually available in
MH or elsewhere on Campus. Workshop, bulk storage, greenhouse production,
animal, utility-plant, and other space-intensive capabilities should be located
where the total workflow works best.

## Serviceability

Service points should be reachable from exterior, mechanical, shared service,
or removable-access zones when practical. Routine service should not require
destructive opening when an economical service path can be designed in.

Future-readiness should favor cheap durable pathways, blocking, conduits,
clearances, and interfaces; replaceable payload/capacity is installed as demand
appears rather than at speculative future maximum.

## Later Life

After the Main House exists, GH should retain useful independent capability
rather than becoming stranded infrastructure. Potential later roles include
family/guest housing, caregiver suite, office/studio, or other legally and
site-appropriate uses, but these are options rather than Day-1 requirements.
