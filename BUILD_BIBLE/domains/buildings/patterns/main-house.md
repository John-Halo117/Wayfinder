# Main House Pattern

The Main House (MH) is the long-term primary residential platform for shelter,
daily life, utilities, safety, storage, serviceability, privacy, recovery, and
adaptation. Approximately 2,700 ft² is a planning baseline, not a hard semantic
requirement.

The MH is **not** an enlarged tiny house and is not required to inherit GH
compression tactics. Its profile spends space intentionally: recurring daily
friction, privacy, acoustics, accessibility, intuitive circulation, delight,
spaciousness, and restorative negative space may legitimately justify area.

## Scope

- Profile: `MH`
- Human-occupied stories: **one**
- No design may depend on stairs, a second occupied story, or inter-floor
  human/cargo/laundry/grocery transfer.
- Overhead or interstitial volume may host qualified non-occupied storage,
  service infrastructure, cat circulation, or deployable capability when
  structure, code, accessibility, clearances, maintenance, and failure recovery
  are satisfactory.

## Required Capabilities

- durable envelope and code-compliant egress
- accessible, legible one-story circulation
- primary building/utility spine
- kitchen, sanitation, sleeping, storage, living, recovery, and hospitality
  support
- water, waste, power, ventilation, lighting, communications, security, and
  monitoring interfaces where available and justified
- maintainable mechanical/service core
- accessible service documentation and non-destructive routine service paths
  where practical
- protected private/restorative zones and acoustic separation
- future expansion interfaces without speculative future-maximum equipment

## Spatial Defaults

- Hallways are allowed and may be desirable when they improve privacy,
  acoustics, navigation, accessibility, public/private zoning, service/egress,
  visual pacing, or architectural experience.
- Reject pointless circulation; do **not** reject hallways categorically.
- Dedicated rooms are allowed where they reduce recurring friction or support
  privacy, acoustics, fixed infrastructure, or simultaneous use.
- High-frequency activities should generally require fewer deploy/reset
  transformations than in GH.
- More permanent furniture and larger storage are acceptable when they improve
  ordinary life rather than merely filling area.
- Walk-in storage is allowed when its internal circulation/access benefit earns
  the area; it is not automatically superior to reach-in or pull-out storage.
- Deliberate transition spaces and generous clearances may be valuable.
- Selective redundancy is allowed when convenience or resilience exceeds its
  lifecycle burden.
- Views, procession, delight, spaciousness, and negative space are legitimate
  design outputs, not inefficiencies that must be compressed away.

## Explicit Non-Goals

Do not optimize MH for minimum square footage, maximum function count, maximum
surface utilization, or maximum transformation density.

Do not import GH rules such as blanket hallway elimination, aggressive
multi-use conversion, or compact-storage preference simply because they reduce
floor area. Use them only when they independently improve the MH requirement.

Do not force workshop, bulk storage, greenhouse production, animal systems,
seasonal equipment, or other noisy/dirty/space-intensive Campus capability into
MH unless workflow, accessibility, frequency, security, or weather exposure
actually makes indoor placement better.

## Serviceability

Critical systems should concentrate around mechanical rooms, utility walls,
service cavities, accessible chases, exterior/shared service zones, crawl/service
volumes where appropriate, or removable assemblies. Do not assume an occupied
upper floor or stair-accessed service story.

Concealed maintainable components need planned access, isolation/disconnect,
inspection, working clearance, removal, and recovery paths. Routine maintenance
should not require destructive wall opening when an economical service path can
be provided.

## Expansion

Expansion should first consume reserved panel capacity, spare conduits,
accessible chases, utility/service walls, planned attachment/connection points,
and other cheap durable interfaces. Installed equipment capacity should follow
observed demand plus justified reserve rather than speculative future maximum.
