# Continuity and Capsules

Continuity is the ability to resume capability across interruption, failure, handoff,
time, replacement, or loss of working context.

Capsules are continuity projections. They are not a second world model, not folders
of summaries, and not manually assembled reports whose value depends on constant
human curation.

## Continuity Loop

The runtime continuity loop is distinct from the repository structure:

Reality -> Observation -> Representation -> Interpretation -> Reasoning -> Views ->
Navigation -> Transformation -> Planning -> Execution -> Reality.

Each stage should preserve enough evidence and provenance that later stages can be
challenged, reconstructed, or resumed without treating a derived representation as
reality itself.

## Context Restoration

Context Restoration is the ability to recover enough relevant state, relationships,
evidence, constraints, commitments, open loops, artifacts, and working context to
continue without reconstructing the entire history.

Compression is allowed; erasure of evidence ancestry is not. A compact re-entry
surface should always remain traceable to the evidence and artifacts from which it
was derived when consequence warrants it.

## Sedimentary Capsules

Capsules accrete from reality over time. Events, observations, decisions,
constraints, artifacts, outputs, outcomes, and unresolved questions form sediment.
Useful sediment compacts into progressively stronger continuity layers.

Canonical lifecycle:

- Stage 0 — Sediment: raw continuity-relevant material exists but has not earned a
  stable re-entry surface.
- Stage 1 — Emerging Capsule: recurring structure or likely future re-entry value is
  visible; the capsule begins to organize itself around evidence and capability.
- Stage 2 — Operational Capsule: a human or system can reliably resume useful work
  from the capsule without reconstructing the entire past.
- Stage 3 — Autonomous Capsule: continuity maintenance, evidence linking, refresh,
  degradation detection, and re-entry preparation are mostly maintained by the
  system while remaining inspectable and reversible.

Promotion depends on demonstrated re-entry value, evidence quality, stability,
retrievability, and maintenance cost. A capsule may degrade or be demoted when its
sources become stale, its assumptions fail, or its maintenance cost exceeds its
continuity value.

## Temporal Sedimentation

Quarterly, seasonal, annual, project, property, system, and life-stage views may be
rendered from the same sedimentary continuity substrate. These are projections, not
separate archives.

The system should preserve accumulated continuity without forcing every old detail
onto the active surface. Older layers may compact while retaining lineage.

## Capsule Density

Capsule Density is recoverable capability per unit retrieval effort. More stored
information is not automatically more continuity. A smaller capsule that restores
useful operation quickly can be superior to a larger archive that requires hours of
reconstruction.

## Continuity Boundaries

Capsules preserve capability and context; ARK preserves observations, evidence,
lineage, and reality records. Jarvis uses Capsules to restore bearings. Neither
Capsules nor Jarvis becomes the authoritative owner of reality merely because they
contain useful compressed representations.

## Theseus Continuity

Continuity should survive substrate replacement. A capability or project may move
between applications, storage systems, models, people, or machines without losing
its identity, evidence ancestry, commitments, and resumption path.

Preserve the invariant. Replace the rest.
