# Universal Capability Harvest & Physical Compression Doctrine

## Purpose

The Build Bible remains the canonical physical design and lifecycle truth store. Polaris may discover, qualify, compare, recommend, approve, orchestrate, and verify capability changes, but external technological progress does not automatically rewrite physical design or authorize construction.

The governing physical question is:

> What changed externally that lets the property satisfy the same requirement with less permanent machinery, embeddedness, maintenance, resource burden, or custom work?

## Requirement-first ladder

For every physical capability descend:

DO NOTHING → USE WHAT IS ALREADY OWNED → USE EXTERNAL CAPABILITY AS-IS → CONFIGURE → ACCESSORIZE → COMBINE → INTEGRATE → ADAPT → EXTEND → CUSTOMIZE → CUSTOM BUILD.

Stop at the first layer that satisfies the actual requirement with acceptable lifecycle burden. Build only the irreducible consequential delta.

REQUIREMENT ≠ FUNCTION ≠ MECHANISM ≠ IMPLEMENTATION.

## Physical adapter law

Prefer:

STANDARD PRODUCT ↔ SIMPLE REPLACEABLE ADAPTER ↔ STANDARD CAMPUS INTERFACE.

Favor standard rails, brackets, couplers, mounting plates, manifolds, quick disconnects, common service clearances, generic conduit/chases, portable controls, and separable utility interfaces. Commodity equipment should not become demolition-grade infrastructure merely because it is current at design time.

Higher embeddedness requires stronger evidence and a higher approval threshold.

## Late binding

Permanent work should normally encode capability envelopes and interfaces rather than premature product SKUs. Re-run external capability harvesting immediately before consequential procurement, rough-in commitments, concealed utility work, and other points where current market capability can still change the design.

A product named during concept design is a candidate satisfier, not a permanent requirement.

## External progress means recompute, not append

When a product, standard, service, contractor capability, rental option, fabrication method, control platform, appliance, machine, material, or building system improves:

1. Re-evaluate the requirement from first principles.
2. Search for existing/commodity capability and composition.
3. Identify what planned or deployed work can disappear.
4. Preserve protected constraints and useful redundancy.
5. Prefer the smallest architecture that still satisfies the requirement.

Do not merely add the new product beside the old design.

## House-plan projection

External capability changes normally alter implementation before geometry. The current one-story GH/MH, consolidated service-spine, finite-access-panel, future-pull-path, restorative-space, accessibility, serviceability, and inspection-before-cover requirements remain unless a verified capability change actually invalidates them.

Strengthen by default:
- replaceable interfaces and service clearances;
- generic power/data/PoE and future conduit reach;
- product-independent mechanical/electrical capacity envelopes;
- removable equipment and accessible shutoffs/disconnects;
- pre-cover photographs and verification;
- decision-time rescanning before purchases and concealed commitments.

Weaken/remove by default when external capability makes them unnecessary:
- vendor-specific permanent geometry;
- proprietary bridge assumptions;
- duplicated controllers/state stores;
- custom domain-control machinery already adequately owned by a specialist;
- premature SKU-specific rough-in;
- parallel infrastructure retained only because it was previously planned.

## Specialized operational ownership

Where adequate, equipment-native controllers and specialized platforms own local operational loops. Polaris owns cross-domain evidence, requirement matching, architecture/approval state, and consequence propagation. The Build Bible owns durable physical design truth. Do not create independent shadow truths.

## Physical approval path

Material or hard-to-reverse physical transitions use:

DESIGN → PROCUREMENT → INSTALLATION READINESS → ROUGH-IN → PRE-COVER INSPECTION → COMMISSIONING → ACCEPTANCE.

Never lose a cheap inspection opportunity through concealment. Photograph or equivalently record concealed utilities and structural interfaces before cover.

Discovery and information enrichment do not confer authority to execute. Structural work, concealed utilities, excavation, destructive removal, major irreversible purchases, and difficult lock-in remain explicit approval transitions with independent second-pass review.

## Deletion-first lifecycle

Every qualified capability jump asks what can be cancelled, postponed, downsized, substituted, consolidated, disabled, sold, repurposed, recycled, or retired. Deletion is a positive lifecycle event only after the replacement is proven. Prefer DISABLE → OBSERVE → DELETE where practical.

Track displaced money, equipment, floor/storage space, power/energy, water/material, maintenance/labor, consumables, spare inventory, service calls, interfaces, failure modes, and cognitive burden where material.

## Rate, time, and provider constraints

Rate limits, time budgets, source/tool outages, and transient provider failures are execution-routing constraints, not objective termination. Reuse still-valid evidence, deduplicate and batch work, prefer native events, checkpoint/resume, substitute equivalent authoritative sources, use bounded backoff, and prioritize decision-sensitive research.

Only nonessential breadth, depth, or frequency may degrade. Never degrade protected constraints, authority/approval requirements, safety, epistemic honesty, source independence where consequential, or required verification. Missing evidence remains UNKNOWN.

## Invariant

Physical reality > prior architecture. Requirements > implementations. External progress should normally reduce embedded complexity and lifecycle burden before it adds technology.
